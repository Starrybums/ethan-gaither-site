The starter website

One site that does its job

For owner-run local businesses. Trades, salons, studios, cafes, shops with a counter.

When someone looks you up on their phone, they should find you, see what you do, and be able to reach you. That's the whole job.

  • Up to five pages

    Home, Services, About and Contact, plus one more that suits you, like a gallery, an FAQ or a service area. A smaller three-page version covers Home, Services and Contact.

  • A design built around you

    I start from your logo and colors. It's built for phones first, then wider screens. Plain, readable and quick.

  • A contact form that reaches you

    Messages go straight to your email through a form service. Basic spam protection is on. There's no custom backend for you to look after.

  • Tap-to-call and a map

    One tap rings you. One tap opens directions. Your hours sit where people look for them.

  • Basic SEO and listings setup

    Page titles, descriptions, headings, alt text, a sitemap, and help with your Google Business Profile. What that covers, and what it doesn't, is spelled out below.

  • Speed and accessibility checks

    Before launch I check contrast, tap targets, keyboard use, alt text, forms, the 404 page and speed. I aim for 90 or better on Lighthouse's mobile test. That's a target, not a promise.

  • A real handover

    The domain, hosting account, files and logins go to you, with a one-page note on how it's set up and how to ask for changes.

Not sure it fits? Read what's not included, then ask. If I'm not the right person, I'll say so.

The process

How it goes

Seven steps. I don't build anything until the scope and the price are agreed.

  1. We talk

    A free call or a few emails. I find out what the business does, who needs to find you, and what the site has to do.

    YouTell me what you have now and what isn't working.

    MeAsk questions, and tell you if I'm the right fit.

  2. You get a price

    A fixed price, agreed before I start, plus WA sales tax. It lists the pages, what's included and what isn't. Nothing starts until you've said yes in writing and paid the first part.

    YouRead it. Ask about anything that isn't clear.

    MeWrite it in plain language, with no hourly meter.

  3. You send your content

    Logo, photos, words, hours, services. The checklist is below. The clock starts when I have your deposit and your content.

    YouSend what you have. Say what's missing.

    MeLay it out, compress the photos, fix typos and unclear lines.

  4. I build the draft

    You get a private link to a working draft you can open on your phone. It's the page itself, not a picture of one.

    YouLook at it on your phone and on a computer.

    MeBuild it by hand, phone first.

  5. You ask for changes

    Two rounds of changes are included. A round is one batch that you send me together. More rounds are quoted first.

    YouSend your changes in one list.

    MeMake them, and tell you what I did.

  6. Checks and launch

    Before launch I check contrast, tap targets, keyboard use, alt text, the 404 page, the form and speed. You pay the rest. I connect your domain and switch it on.

    YouPay the balance. Make sure the domain is in your name.

    MeRun every check, fix what fails, go live.

  7. Handover and the fix window

    You get the logins, the files and a one-page note on how the site is set up and how to ask for changes. For 14 days after launch, I fix anything that doesn't work the way we agreed.

    YouKeep the logins somewhere safe.

    MeMove every account to your name and hand over the files.

What you send me

What I need from you

The more of this you have ready, the faster it goes.

  • Your logo, in any file you have. I'll tell you if I need a better one.
  • Your business name, address, phone, email and hours, written the way they should appear.
  • Photos of your work, your space or your team. Ones you took or own.
  • The words for each page, even rough ones. I fix typos and unclear lines. I don't write pages from scratch.
  • A list of your services or products.
  • Links to any listings you already have, such as Google or Facebook.
  • Anything you don't want on the site.

Missing some of it? Say so on the call. Photography, logo design and writing from scratch aren't part of the starter site.

SEO and listings

Getting found, without the promises

People look you up on Google, mostly on their phones. The aim is that they find the right information.

For a local business, the map listing often matters as much as the website. That's why listings setup is part of the work.

What I do

  • Write a clear title and description for every page.
  • Use proper headings and alt text, so search engines and screen readers can follow each page.
  • Add structured data: the code that tells Google your name, address, phone, hours and services.
  • Make a sitemap, set up Google Search Console in your name, and send Google the sitemap.
  • Make the site fast on a phone.
  • Help you claim and fill in your Google Business Profile. Google checks with you directly that the business is yours, and I walk you through it. The profile belongs to you.
  • Check that your name, address and phone match everywhere you're listed.

What I can't do

  • Promise you a spot on Google. Nobody outside Google can. Anyone who promises page one or number one is guessing, or selling something.
  • Control where you rank. That depends on your competition, your Business Profile, what your customers say about you, and time.
  • Do ongoing SEO as part of the starter site. Monthly content, link building and rank tracking are a separate job. Ask me, and I'll tell you if it's worth it for your business.

Ownership

You own everything

When we're done, nothing important is in my name. If I disappeared tomorrow, your site would keep running and any other developer could pick it up.

  • Domain name

    In your name. Registered with a registrar you choose. You pay them directly, and you can move it whenever you like.

  • Hosting

    In your name. A hosting account you own. You pay the host directly. I put the site on it.

  • The code

    Yours. You get every file, plus a copy in a repository you own. It's plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, so there's no page builder or framework to be locked into.

  • Logins

    Yours. Hosting, domain, form service, Search Console, your Business Profile, and analytics if you want it. I move each one to your name, then remove my own access.

  • Your content

    Yours. The photos, words and logo you send are yours before the project and after it.

Not included

What the starter site doesn't cover

I'd rather you hear it now than find out later.

  • Online stores, payments and booking systems. One of my sample sites has a working demo cart. A real store is a different project.
  • Logins, member areas and custom databases.
  • Heavy custom animation, 3D and video backgrounds. This site has them. They're quoted separately.
  • A dashboard for editing the site yourself. Changes go through me.
  • Ongoing hosting, updates and security, unless you take the care plan below.
  • Writing pages from scratch, photography, logo or brand design.
  • Extra pages and extra rounds of changes. I quote them first.
  • Ongoing SEO or ranking work.
  • Your domain, hosting and any paid tools. You pay those directly.

Need one of these? Say so on the call. I'll quote it separately, or tell you who to ask.

After launch

When something needs fixing

  • The 14-day fix window

    For 14 days after launch, I fix anything that doesn't work the way we agreed, at no extra cost. A new request is a change, not a fix. I quote changes first.

  • Changes later

    Ask me whenever you need one. I tell you what it costs before I start, so there's no surprise bill.

  • The care plan

    It's optional. Each month I check your hosting, run updates and keep a backup, and I make small changes. Ask me for the price and I'll send it with your quote.
